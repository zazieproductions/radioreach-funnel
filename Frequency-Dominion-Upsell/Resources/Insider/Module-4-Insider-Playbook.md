## THE ADD-CYCLE CLOCK (SCHEDULE YOUR CAMPAIGN INSIDE RADIO'S RHYTHM)

Radio acts on cycles, not on your energy. Align the 21 days to the industry calendar:

| Day | Industry reality | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Rotation boards refresh; add decisions from Friday reads | Load + verify (no sends) |
| **Tue** | New-music Tuesday: most commercial add dates | Wave 1 — first half of the list |
| **Wed–Thu** | College MDs process; specialty hosts plan weekends | Wave 2; touch-2 value to wave 1 |
| **Fri** | Week's rotation locks; specialty show prep | Log; no cold pitches after 11am |
| **Sat–Sun** | Specialty shows air; hosts are reachable and *excited* | Host DMs, thank-yous, value drops |
| **Next Mon** | Fresh rotation board | Touch-3 grace closes land on a fresh week |

**The insider rule:** the campaign is a *weekly machine*, not a 21-day sprint. Each week of the campaign rides one rotation cycle. If you fire wave 1 on a Thursday and wave 2 on a Friday afternoon, you've just burned two weeks of cycles in one send — which is why the Load → Tue/Thu → next-Monday rhythm is the actual operating system.

## THE IMPACT-DATE MOVE (HOW LABELS TIME A CAMPAIGN)

Labels don't tell a station "this is out." They tell the station **"this is the impact date — play it the week of [date]."** That frames the add as a *scheduled event*, not a favor:

> "Official impact: [date]. Rotation-ready. We're servicing [NUMBER] markets that week."

**Your version:** give your campaign an internal impact date. Announce it in the pitch — "servicing [city] stations the week of [date]" — and time wave 1 exactly 1–2 weeks *before* the impact so the add lands *on* the week. PDs are calendar animals; a date converts "maybe" into "when?"

## THE COLLEGE CHART EXPRESS (SERVICE → ADDS → CHART → SECOND WAVE)

This is the mechanic the whole campaign optimizes for:

1. **Service** (wk 1): 10 college slots + 4 specialty — the Tier-1 allocation.
2. **Adds** (wk 2): rotation boards pick up; you track adds daily in T4.4.
3. **Chart** (wk 3): college stations report to NACC/RMR. Chart position is *evidence*.
4. **Second wave** (wk 4): the chart listing becomes the proof line for every commercial and mid-metro pitch: *"charting at [NUMBER] college stations, NACC top [NUMBER]."*

Run this loop and you're doing what labels pay $2,000+ per record for — with 20 stations and 21 days. The first add's real job is to start the chart. The chart's real job is to make the next 20 stations easy.

## THE ROTATION-UPGRADE STRATEGY (THE KILLER FOLLOW-UP)

Most artists treat a light-rotation add as the end. Operators treat it as the **start of the upgrade campaign**:

- **Week after the add:** thank-you + listener-feedback receipts (streams, tickets, requests).
- **Two weeks after:** second receipt — usually market-specific ("your metro streams +30%").
- **Three weeks after:** the upgrade ask: *"Is there a path for [SONG] to move into medium? We can support with [asset]."*

The upgrade ask is the single most winnable ask in the industry — it's a *category change*, not a new favor — and it's the one almost no independent ever makes.

## THE SPIN-TRACKING DISCIPLINE (WHAT LABELS CHASE)

Spins are the industry's raw score. Track weekly per station: `adds`, `spins`, `rotation category`, `chart position`. The total spin count is your **momentum number** — the number that appears on the second-wave pitch, the sync package, the sponsor deck, and the booking email. Without weekly spin tracking, you're running a campaign without a scoreboard. With it, you're running a business.

## THE 3-TOUCH ESCALATION PSYCHOLOGY (THE INSIDER VERSION)

- **Touch 1:** the ask lands. Silence = the PD is *not deciding yet* (busy, not hostile).
- **Touch 2 (value):** the receipt or asset gives them a *new reason*. This is where the "busy but interested" cohort converts.
- **Touch 3 (grace):** the close. Politeness + an open door. This is where the "I need a deadline" cohort converts — and where the "no" cohort converts into a *relationship* instead of a burn.

The system's secret: all three touches are about the *next campaign* too. Touch 3's grace close is what makes touch 1 of your next release warmer than touch 3 of this one.

## THE ONE SECRET MOST ARTISTS NEVER LEARN

**Adds happen in waves, not trickles.** If you see 2–3 adds land in one morning, the rest of the week's board is likely moving too — that's the rotation cycle turning. Watch the wave: when it turns, escalate the second-wave pitch to every station that *hasn't* added yet, with the fresh proof. Timing the second wave off the first wave's momentum is the difference between 5 adds and 11.
