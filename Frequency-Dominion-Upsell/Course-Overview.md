## THE DOCTRINE

Most artists treat radio like a favor they're hoping to collect. They buy a contact list, blast a generic email to 18,000 inboxes, and call it promotion. A list is not a strategy. A blast is not a campaign. And hoping is not a business model.

Frequency Dominion is the opposite operating system:

> **You do not ask for airplay. You own the frequency.**

An owner does three things a beggar can never do:

1. **He chooses his targets like a buyer, not a suitor.** Every station is a customer with a measurable need, a format, a clock, and a competition. You serve the need — or you don't touch the station.
2. **He proves value before he asks for it.** Proof stacks, EPKs, and 90-second audio weapons mean the Program Director's first impression is *"this is already a professional"* — not *"another artist wants my time."*
3. **He runs campaigns, not emails.** 21-day load sequences, 3-touch follow-ups, expansion loops, and 90-day audits. Systems compound. Emails don't.

## THE SEVEN LAWS OF FREQUENCY DOMINION

1. **The Unheard Tax is real.** Every week without airplay costs you reach, revenue, and leverage. That tax is the enemy. This course is the audit.
2. **A station is a customer, not a favor.** Serve the PD's clock. Win the format. Respect the market.
3. **Proof precedes the pitch.** Never ask a stranger to take a risk on an unknown. Show the proof stack first.
4. **Targets are architecture, not luck.** Hometown heat map → format score → 20-station war room → campaign. In that order. Every time.
5. **The pitch is a weapon, not a message.** 90 words. One hook. One ask. Radio-ready audio in the first email.
6. **Follow-up is a system, not a feeling.** Three touches, spaced, escalated, polite. Persistence without the stench.
7. **Empires compound.** Proof → adds → exposure → income → better proof → bigger stations. The loop is the business.
