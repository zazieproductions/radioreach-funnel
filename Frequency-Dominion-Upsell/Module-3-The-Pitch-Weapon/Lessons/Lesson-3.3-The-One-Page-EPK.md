# 3.3 The One-Page EPK That Commands Attention

> **THE ONE-LINE TRUTH:** Your EPK is not a bio PDF — it's a one-page command deck that answers every question a PD has in 60 seconds, so the only question left is yes.

## VIDEO SCRIPT (ready to record · runtime ≈ 09:00)

**[0:00 – 0:50] COLD OPEN — face to camera.**
"Right now your press kit is somewhere: a PDF, a linktree, a bio on three different sites, a website with a music player that takes 40 seconds to load. A PD with four seconds to decide is not doing archaeology. The one-page EPK is a *command deck* — everything they need, one page, 60 seconds to evaluate."

**[0:50 – 2:20] THE ONE-PAGE LAW — screen-share the layout.**
"One page. Six blocks. Block one: the face — one photo that looks like a professional. Block two: the name + one-line genre description. Block three: the proof — three receipts max, third-party, market-specific. Block four: the music — one 90-second embed + one radio edit, no playlist required. Block five: the press + logos, three max. Block six: the logistics — contact, city, tour dates, socials, streaming links. That's it. Anything that doesn't fit gets cut. The one-page law is the reason it converts."

**[2:20 – 4:00] THE 60-SECOND PD TEST — the evaluation sequence.**
"Here's the test. A PD opens your link, and in 60 seconds they answer five questions. One: is this a real artist? Two: what do they sound like in 10 seconds? Three: has anyone else believed in them? Four: what's the story — local, dates, buzz? Five: who do I contact? If your page forces them to scroll, click, download, or search — you failed. Every block must answer exactly one question with zero navigation."

**[4:00 – 5:40] THE COMMAND VERSION — how one page beats a website.**
"Your website is for fans. Your EPK is for decision-makers. Different job, different design. An EPK's job: instant authority. Use a dark, professional, print-quality layout. Real photo, no filters. One genre line: 'Genre: dark indie pop.' Not 'genre-fluid, genre-bending, impossible to define' — decision-makers hate puzzles. Proof with numbers. Press with logos. Dates with venues. If it looks like a label built it, it gets treated like a label. If it looks like a SoundCloud page, it gets treated like a hobby."

**[5:40 – 7:20] THE DATA-FILLED EPK — the receipts inside.**
"Every block pulls from your Proof Stack (Lesson 1.3). Block three — the receipts: '2,100 monthly listeners · 450 tickets sold · featured in [press].' One line per receipt, facts only. Block five — press logos you can verify. The EPK is the stack, rendered. That's why the proof vault is a weapon and not a folder."

**[7:20 – 8:10] THE DISTRIBUTION RULES — where the one page lives.**
"Three copies: (1) the URL in your email — always, never attached; (2) the QR code on your flyers and at merch tables — yes, for venue bookers too; (3) the mobile-first version — 80% of PDs will open it on a phone. Test on a phone. If it requires pinch-to-zoom, you're dead."

**[8:10 – 9:00] CLOSE — module card.**
"Proof deck done. Now the weapon that makes them *listen* — the radio-ready audio that forces replies. Next lesson."

## IMPLEMENTATION DRILLS

- **Drill 1 — The Six-Block Audit (30 min).** Open your current press materials. Check them against the six blocks. **Pass standard:** every block either exists or you've named its replacement. If you have three different EPKs, that's the answer.
- **Drill 2 — The Command Deck Build (2–4 hrs).** Build your one-page EPK with `Templates/T3.3-One-Page-EPK-Brief.csv` as your content map, or use `Bonuses/Bonus-3-EPK-Command-Deck/EPK-Command-Deck.html` as your production-ready template. **Pass standard:** one page, six blocks, zero dead links, 60-second PD test passes.
- **Drill 3 — The 60-Second Test (15 min).** Time a stranger (or your group) on the five PD questions. **Pass standard:** all five answered in under 60 seconds without leaving the page.
- **Drill 4 — The Mobile Check (10 min).** Open your EPK on a phone, one thumb, no pinch-zoom. **Pass standard:** every block readable and every button tappable.

**WAR-ROOM NOTES — 3.3 The One-Page EPK**

**THE EPK IS A DECISION INTERFACE, NOT ART**

Most EPKs are a museum: beautiful, slow, and irrelevant to the person in a hurry. A decision interface is the opposite — it minimizes the time to a confident "yes" or a clean "no." Every element that doesn't answer a PD question is a tax on that decision. Design rule: if a PD can't answer all five questions in 60 seconds, the page is a wall, not a door.

**THE THREE-RECEIPT CURATION RULE**

The Proof Stack may hold 90 items. The EPK carries three — and only three — because three is the number a busy professional actually reads. Choose by *market relevance*, not by impressiveness: a Portland PD wants Portland receipts; an Austin PD wants Austin receipts. Variant EPKs per region are the mark of an operator. One master deck, three regional editions, refreshed quarterly.

**THE GENRE LINE IS A FILTER, NOT A CAPTION**

Amateur EPKs say "genre-fluid," "unclassifiable," or list eight genres. Operators say "genre: dark indie pop" — a sentence that lets the PD sort instantly and gives them the *words to defend the add to their team*. A PD who can say "it's an indie pop track with a local story" can sell you internally. A PD who can't describe your genre can't sell you at all. Clarity is an add-on: the genre line is a decision-support tool.

**THE IMAGE HIERARCHY: PROOF, NOT PRETTY**

Your photo should communicate *stage presence* (live shot, legit lights, crowd) over *studio aesthetics* (beauty shot with a mic). A live photo with an actual audience is a receipt; a studio pose is a wish. Use the live photo. Logos only if verifiable — an unverifiable press logo costs more credibility than it earns. Numbers with sources always beat adjectives with none.

**THE LINK-DISCIPLINE: NEVER ATTACH THE EPK**

Attachments get quarantined, get deleted, and die in phone apps. The EPK URL is a *proof-of-professionalism* move: it signals that you understand how decision-makers consume information — quickly, on mobile, with zero friction. Always send: (1) one radio edit, (2) one 90-second audio, (3) the EPK link. Nothing else. That's the payload that gets replies.
