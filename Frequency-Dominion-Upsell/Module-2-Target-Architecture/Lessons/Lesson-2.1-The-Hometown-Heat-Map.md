# 2.1 The Hometown Heat Map — Find the Stations That Can't Say No

> **THE ONE-LINE TRUTH:** Your first 20 wins are a geography problem, not a music problem. The heat map shows you where the airplay is closest, cheapest, and most winnable.

## IMPLEMENTATION DRILLS

- **Drill 1 — The Heat Map Sprint (60 min).** Open `Templates/T2.1-Hometown-Heat-Map.csv` (also Bonus 1 database). Find and log 60 stations within 300 miles. **Pass standard:** 60 rows, names + markets + a contact for at least 15.
- **Drill 2 — The Ring Cut (20 min).** Tag each station by ring 1–4, format, and platform. **Pass standard:** every row has both tags.
- **Drill 3 — The Winner Zone Sort (20 min).** Filter to ring 1–2 + format-compatible + contact available. Rank top 30. **Pass standard:** a numbered list of 30 with two sentences each — why this station, what's the story.
- **Drill 4 — The Local Story Draft (30 min).** Write a 3-sentence local story per top-10 station: "Artist is from [city], [accomplishment], [local tie]." **Pass standard:** all 10, no placeholders.

**WAR-ROOM NOTES — 2.1 The Hometown Heat Map**

**GEOGRAPHY IS THE MOST UNDERUSED ADVANTAGE IN INDEPENDENT MUSIC**

Labels spend 90% of radio effort on national commercial stations where the independent win rate is near zero. The winnable inventory — local, college, community, online, specialty — is almost entirely unclaimed by artists who are too proud to play the "local" card. Local is not a consolation prize. Local is the *entry point* to every national story: "the artist the region is talking about" travels further than "an artist who emailed us."

**RING THEORY — THE DISTANCE DECAY OF ACCESS**

Your probability of a "yes" from an in-person relationship beats an email-only relationship by roughly 10x, and every 30 minutes of driving expands the relationship surface: drop-ins, show tickets, guest lists, handshakes. Ring 1 (home) and Ring 2 (≤1 hour) are relationship zones, not email zones. Ring 3 (≤2 hours) is a hybrid: email first, drop-in on release week. Ring 4 (≤300 miles) is your tour corridor: pre-roll when dates exist (Lesson 2.2). Do not pitch Ring 4 without dates. It's a waste of ammunition.

**THE FOUR SEGMENTATION CUTS (IN ORDER)**

1. Ring — home first, always.
2. Format — score it (Lesson 2.3).
3. Platform — online stations add on process, not politics: fastest proof machine.
4. Story — local angle, show dates, community ties. The story column is what converts a format match into a yes.

**THE "CAN'T SAY NO" TRIANGLE**

The perfect first target has three legs: (a) a format that fits, (b) a local story they'd be embarrassed to ignore, (c) a human contact who has seen you (or your proof). Two legs = warm. Three legs = winnable. Only map stations that have at least two legs before you spend a single drill on them.

**THE MAP IS A LAND-BANK, NOT A TO-DO LIST**

You will not pitch 300 stations. You will *own* a map of 300. The map lets you choose the right 20 today, the next 20 in 90 days, and the market you enter in 6 months — with the research already done. Build it once. Farm it forever.
