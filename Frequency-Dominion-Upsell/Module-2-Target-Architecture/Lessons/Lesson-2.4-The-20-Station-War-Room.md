# 2.4 The 20-Station War Room — Build the Command Center That Never Sleeps

> **THE ONE-LINE TRUTH:** Dominion is concentration of force. A locked war room of 20 scored, personalized stations will produce 5x more adds than an undisciplined database of 500 names.

## MASTERCLASS DIRECTIVE & STRATEGIC BLUEPRINT

### Strategic Foundation & Operational Reality
Amateur artists believe that successful radio promotion is a numbers game governed by sheer volume. They buy contact lists of 5,000 email addresses, copy-paste a generic pitch, and hit send. Within 72 hours, their email deliverability collapses, zero meaningful adds materialize, and the artist collapses in exhaustion, convinced that radio is an impenetrable cartel.

Dominion operators operate under the military principle of **Concentration of Force**.

Instead of spreading thin across hundreds of unvetted contacts, you concentrate your operational bandwidth on a tightly curated **20-Station War Room**. Twenty stations is the exact mathematical sweet spot for an independent operator:
- Large enough to guarantee statistical airplay yield (a 20% to 30% add conversion rate yields 4 to 6 solid station adds).
- Small enough that every single gatekeeper receives deep, tailored personalization, individual track analysis, and meticulous multi-touch follow-up.
- Fully manageable within 45 minutes of daily operational execution.

### The Anatomy of the 20-Station War Room
Your War Room is not a passive spreadsheet; it is an active tactical cockpit. Every station in your War Room advances through a rigid **6-Stage Lifecycle Ladder**:
1. **Queued (Stage 1):** Scored >= 75 points. Named gatekeeper verified. 3-layer subject line drafted. Audio package staged.
2. **Dispatched (Stage 2):** Touch 1 payload delivered during optimal morning programming window. Timestamp logged.
3. **Engaged / Opened (Stage 3):** Payload opened or link clicked. Monitored via privacy-compliant analytics.
4. **Dialogue / In Review (Stage 4):** Gatekeeper replied, requested assets, or acknowledged submission. Immediate high-value reply dispatched.
5. **Confirmed Add / Spin (Stage 5):** Track placed in rotation, scheduled for specialty play, or booked for an on-air interview. Spinitron confirmation captured.
6. **Retired / Re-Check (Stage 6):** Polite decline received, or 3-touch sequence completed without engagement. Transferred to 90-day re-check calendar.

### The Daily 45-Minute Battle Rhythm
To operate your War Room with peak efficiency, execute this daily protocol four days per week (Monday through Thursday):
- *Minute 0 – 10 (Reconnaissance):* Review Spinitron and playlist logs for target stations to check for spontaneous spins. Log all incoming replies.
- *Minute 10 – 25 (Execution Wave):* Dispatch scheduled Touch 1 initial sends or Touch 2/3 follow-ups strictly per the campaign calendar.
- *Minute 25 – 40 (Asset & Dialogue Response):* Respond to programmer inquiries, provide clean broadcast WAV files, coordinate interview windows, and dispatch custom station sweepers.
- *Minute 40 – 45 (Ledger Reconciliation):* Update every status column, log next-touch dates, and audit pipeline velocity.
