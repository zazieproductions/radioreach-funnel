# 2.2 Tour Pre-Roll — Manufacture Airplay Demand With Dates

> **THE ONE-LINE TRUTH:** A show date is the single strongest airplay argument in independent music — it converts "please play me" into "here's a crowd that's about to exist in your market."

## VIDEO SCRIPT (ready to record · runtime ≈ 08:30)

**[0:00 – 0:45] COLD OPEN — live footage, crowd, then face to camera.**
"Here's what turns a Program Director's head faster than any song: a date. A show. A crowd. A reason for *their* listeners to care right now. You don't need to be famous to manufacture this — you need a date on a calendar."

**[0:45 – 2:00] WHY DATES ARE RADIO FUEL — on-screen: "DATES = STORY + LISTENERS + DEADLINE".**
"A show gives a PD three things at once. Story: 'local artist, playing the [venue] on the 14th, ticket link.' Listeners: every listener who comes to that show is *their* audience — you're bringing them a crowd that already listens to their station. Deadline: 'we'd love to hear it before the show' puts a timer on the decision, which moves PDs from maybe to yes. No date, no story, no deadline, no add. It's that simple."

**[2:00 – 3:30] THE PRE-ROLL ASSET — the 60-second video clip.**
"The asset: a 60-second video — 10 seconds of live footage, 10 seconds of crowd, 20 seconds of the best song moment, 20 seconds with date, venue, ticket link on screen. This is not a music video. It's a *radio promo* — built for the PD's second brain, exactly the way a label pre-rolls a market. Post it. Send it. It makes the pitch a delivery, not an ask."

**[3:30 – 5:00] THE PRE-ROLL CAMPAIGN — screen-share template T2.2.**
"Timeline. T-21: announce the date — post everywhere, add to heat map story column. T-14: create the pre-roll asset. T-10: first pre-roll send to the market's top 5 stations — 90-word pitch + video + proof stack. T-7: follow-up touch two — the 'local crowd is coming' email. T-4: touch three — final 'come be part of it.' T-0 show night: invite the PD personally. That's the sequence. Run it for every date you play. Every. Single. One."

**[5:00 – 6:40] THE FALSE MARKET — the discipline.**
"There's a trap: not every date is radio fuel. A coffee-shop 20-seater in a town with no stations isn't a market — don't pitch it. The rule: a date only counts if (1) it's inside your heat map, (2) you can get a ticket list, and (3) you can point to a real room. No fake rooms, no 'we'll say we're touring' lines. PDs can smell manufactured demand from a hundred miles. Real dates. Real numbers."

**[6:40 – 7:40] THE CROWD TELEGRAM — what to send after the show.**
"Forty-eight hours after any show: send the crowd telegram. Date, venue, attendance, photos, video, ticket revenue, and one line of listener feedback. This is the most underused proof weapon in the industry — a PD who sees 300 people at your show is a PD who adds you. Every show becomes a receipt (Lesson 1.3), a story, and a relationship deposit."

**[7:40 – 8:30] CLOSE — module card.**
"Dates are the accelerant. Next: the format scoring system — rating every station on your map like a radio analyst, so you never waste a bullet on a wall."

## WAR-ROOM NOTES

**WAR-ROOM NOTES — 2.2 Tour Pre-Roll**

**THE SHOW IS A MARKETING OBJECT, NOT A PERFORMANCE**

The amateur treats the show as an event. The operator treats it as a *proof-manufacturing machine* with four outputs: ticket receipts (Audience), crowd footage (Story), a market tie (Geography), and a deadline (Urgency). Go into every show knowing which four outputs you're building. If you can't produce all four, the date is not radio fuel — book the date anyway, but don't pitch it.

**THE T-21 TIMELINE IS OPTIMAL, NOT ARBITRARY**

- T-21 to T-14: PDs process local stories on a 2-week cycle. Announcing too early loses urgency; too late loses the chance to book the artist on a show block or interview.
- T-10 first send: your email lands *after* the announcement has circulated, so the PD sees a market story, not a pitch.
- T-7 second touch: escalate with a new asset (crowd video clip, artist-on-radio-adjacent content) — value, not a reminder.
- T-4 third touch: the final ask. After that, stop (per the Oath).
- Show night: the personal handshake. This is where the deep relationship is built (Module 5).

**THE "DRAW THE CROWD" AGENT**

Three ways to make a show a market event without a touring budget: (1) local press mini-blitz — 3 outlets will cover a local show for free; (2) artists' alliance — co-bill with a local act that already pulls crowds; (3) a "listener discount" posted to the station's community page the week of — it makes the station a *partner* in the event rather than a passive recipient of a pitch. All three are done in 3 hours and pay for themselves in story capital.

**THE PRE-ROLL ASSET STANDARD**

One asset, three versions: 60s landscape (email), 30s vertical (reels/stories), 15s bumper (DM attachments). Always with the date as the hero — not the song. Radio cares about the event; the song is the soundtrack.

**THE ANTI-FALSE-MARKET CHECK**

A date is only radio fuel if you can answer three questions: (1) What's the room? (2) How many people showed last time? (3) Which station's listeners would drive to it? Three "I don't knows" = don't pitch. Keep the date, kill the pitch.
