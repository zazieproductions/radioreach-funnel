> **THE ONE-LINE TRUTH:** The first send is a door knock. The second is proof you're serious. The third is the close. Amateurs send once; operators run a 3-touch system — and 80%+ of adds come after the first touch.

**WAR-ROOM NOTES — 4.3 The 3-Touch Follow-Up System**

**THE CUMULATIVE-RESPONSE CURVE (WHY 3 TOUCHES, NOT 1, NOT 5)**

Cold-outreach response curves in radio behave predictably: touch 1 gets its share; touch 2 recovers the "busy but interested" cohort; touch 3 recovers the "I need a deadline" cohort. Beyond touch 3, the marginal conversion collapses to near-zero while the well-burn risk rises sharply — a fourth touch converts the *negative* direction: PDs remember being chased, and the memory persists for years. Three touches is not a suggestion; it's the mathematically optimal persistence policy for relationship-preserving outreach.

**THE TOUCH ROTATION RULE: NEVER REPEAT AN ASSET**

Each touch must present new value. If touch 2 repeats touch 1's audio link, you've sent a reminder — which reads as a no-value message and costs you credibility. Rotate: touch 1 = the pitch + audio; touch 2 = new proof (crowd video, spin receipt, milestone); touch 3 = the graceful close with a new but tiny asset (a 15-sec vertical clip or a follow-up 30-sec cutdown). Repeating the same file is the amateur tell.

**THE DAY-4 / DAY-8 TIMING IS A MECHANISM, NOT A FEELING**

Day 4 lands after the PD's first decision window (busy Monday, pitched Tuesday, processed Wednesday, freed Thursday). Day 8 crosses the weekly-retention cliff (most stray pitches are forgotten within a week). Day 14 (after touch 3) is where the "no-answer" station becomes a clean MONITOR with a 90-day recheck — not a graveyard of hope. The dates are chosen to compress the decision cycle, not to be polite.

**THE "JUST FOLLOWING UP" BAN**

Two words that signal amateur: "just following up." It implies you have nothing new, which is true. Every follow-up in this system is a *proof update*, a *milestone update*, or a *grace close* — never a reminder. If you have nothing new, don't send. The third touch is the only exception, and it's engineered to close, not to nag.

**REPLY SPEED IS A RELATIONSHIP CURRENCY**

Any touch or reply is a *loan of trust*; the PD decides whether you're worth a second loan based on how you handled the first. Responding to a PD's question in under 12 hours signals professional-grade operating tempo — the same tempo that makes them forward your record to a co-worker. Slow replies read as a hobby. Speed is the cheapest proof of professionalism you own.
