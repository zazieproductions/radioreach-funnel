# M4L4 — Days 15–21: adds, interviews, next market

**Module 04 · The 21-Day Campaign** · 8 min lesson (1,041 words of spoken script ≈ 8 min at teleprompter pace) · **Campaign days 15–21**

**Outcome:** Convert every reply into a calendar fact, close the campaign clean, and load market two.

---

## Voiceover script (read this one — teleprompter / VO)

The last week. No new sends — the cadence is spent and the quiet is honored. This week has three jobs: convert, close, and clone. Do them in order and the campaign pays out for years.

Convert first, because a reply is not a win — I'll keep saying that sentence until it's tattooed on your forearm. A reply becomes a win exactly one way: by turning into a calendar fact. An add is a spin with a date and a time you've confirmed in writing. An interview is a scheduled slot with a duration and a topic list. A "send me the 30" is a file delivered and logged. A no is a thanked, calendared, held-open door. Each of those is a noun the ledger can hold; everything else is weather. Your week opens with a conversion pass down the war room: every live thread gets its noun, in writing, this week.

The add, when it comes, triggers a same-day sequence. One: confirm in writing — date, show, time if they offered it. "Friday the 27th, The Night Signal, 10pm block — thank you, Dara" — thirty seconds, and it protects you both against the fog of busy people. Two: the thank-you email goes out the same day, station credited first, zero asks attached. Three: the social post, if they allow it, credits the station before it celebrates you. Four: on spin night, you screenshot everything — the chart page, the stream metadata, whatever exists — and it goes straight into the proof ledger, because chart pages rot and screenshots are the only permanent medium radio offers. In the example, the spin airs March 27th at 10:42pm, and the screenshot becomes proof line number two, which rewrites the EPK's proof block by April.

The interview, when it books, is next week's Module 05 curriculum — but the conversion law applies now: slot, duration, format, and your five answers under forty seconds, prepared the day before, not improvised in the parking lot. And when it airs, same discipline: clip the pull-quote, log it, ledger it. In the example, Marcus records Marisol on day sixteen; it airs day twenty-one, the campaign's closing image — one add on the wall, one interview on the air, one market loaded for next month. That's what a finished first campaign looks like. Not confetti. Inventory.

Close, job two. Day eighteen is the audit — the full numbers pass, which I'll teach properly in Module 06 but run once here because closing a campaign without numbers is how myths replace facts. Sends, touches, replies, adds, interviews, nos, burned wells. In the example: twenty sends, twelve Touch 2s, nine Touch 3s, four human replies, one add, one interview, one imaging ask, one clean no, zero burned wells, fourteen silences. Write yours in the log's audit field. Two sentences, no adjectives, just the shape of the machine's output. Then day twenty-one's closing ritual: every champion gets a final thank-you that isn't a hook for an ask; every nurture row gets a next-touch date for the next release; every dead row gets its reason recorded; the war room comes off the wall, dated, filed with the log. The room is cleared. You'll thank me in June, when the next campaign starts with an empty wall instead of a shrine.

And the emotional close matters as much as the administrative one, so let me coach it. A first campaign that produced one add and a wall of silence can feel like a shrug if you expected a parade. Set the record straight with real numbers: twenty doors, four humans engaged, one of them put your song on the air, one put your voice on the air, and all four now know an artist in their market who does it right. That is a phenomenal return on ten hours of structured work, and it is the floor, not the ceiling — because the next campaign doesn't start from zero. It starts from proof.

Which is job three: clone. Day nineteen in the example, Marisol loads market two — Columbus and Louisville, fifteen stations, KCYQ first because the fall add made it a warm door, the rest pulled from the Vault with the same heat-map method, same scoring, same cut list. Your clone should take one focused session, not a week: the method is already built, the assets exist, the pitch needs one proof line refreshed — which the ledger just handed you. Market two's Day 1 goes on the calendar before market one's war room comes off the wall, and that adjacency is the whole empire loop in miniature: close, learn, clone, climb.

One more conversion artifact, the quietest and most valuable: the pitch rewrite. Not mid-campaign — now, at close, with data. The example's version two changes one line: proof line one becomes "now spinning on 89.1 WVMR," because that's what week three bought. That single edit upgrades every send in market two, and it cost one sentence. That's what proof compounding feels like from inside — not fireworks, just a permanently higher floor.

And one edge case to pre-decide, because it arrives for everyone eventually: the almost. "Great track — we just wrapped adds for this month" or "love it, but we're all-local in October." An almost is a nurture with a date attached, and the conversion noun is the calendared re-pitch, not a maybe. Thank them, ask nothing further, and put the re-pitch on the calendar with the fact they gave you — because a station that told you when its door opens has done your targeting for you. Almosts are the first assets of the next cycle. Log them like the gifts they are.

The drill, in the closing week: run the conversion pass (every thread gets its noun in writing), the audit (numbers into the log), and the clone (market two's fifteen rows loaded, Day 1 calendared). Done is three artifacts: a completed send log with audit notes, a proof ledger with at least one new row, and market two's war room printed and queued. First campaign closed. The loop is loaded. Module 05 is about the humans you just met — the calls, the interviews, and the repairs — because the relationships are the actual product.

```
Conversion nouns: ADD = show + date + time confirmed in writing · INTERVIEW = slot + duration + 5 answers prepped · FILE ASK = delivered + logged · NO = thanked + next-cycle date.

```

## Common failure → fix

**Fails:** The add airs, everyone celebrates on social, and nobody confirms details in writing or screenshots the chart — the win evaporates into vibes.

**Fix:** Same-day add protocol: written confirmation, thank-you, station-first post, screenshot to ledger. A win that isn't logged is a story; logged, it's ammunition for market two's pitch.
