# M4L3 — Days 8–14: the 3-touch follow-up

**Module 04 · The 21-Day Campaign** · 9 min lesson (1,100 words of spoken script ≈ 8 min at teleprompter pace) · **Campaign days 8–14**

**Outcome:** Run Touch 2 and Touch 3 with new information only — the cadence that turned silence into adds.

---

## Voiceover script (read this one — teleprompter / VO)

Week two is where campaigns are won, because week two is where most artists quit. The silence has had eight days to speak, and it said nothing, and the amateur translation of nothing is "they hated it." The operator's translation is different: nothing means not-yet. This week you run the follow-up cadence that the whole architecture has been holding in reserve — two more touches, each one carrying something new, each one a reason to be welcome rather than a weight to be endured.

Touch Two, day eight to ten. Non-responders only — anyone who has replied or cleanly no'd is out of this sequence and into relationship mode. The payload is the 30-second hook. That's deliberate: your first email asked for three and a half minutes of their life, and they didn't spend it. So Touch 2 asks for thirty seconds — the chorus erupting, the cold ending, done. The subject line is the hook line from your three keepers. The body is two sentences and a link: "A 30-second version if that's easier. Still a fit for the show before the date?" plus one line of honest warmth about what their show does. Then a full stop, and the second thing Touch 2 carries: permission. "Either way" — meaning either way, you go quiet and let them off. The exit clause again. It's not a trick; it's the emotional architecture of being easy to resolve.

Touch Three, day fourteen. The last touch of the campaign, and its law is absolute: new information only. Not a nudge, not "bumping this," not "just making sure" — every one of those phrases tells the recipient their silence was a mistake you're correcting, and nobody thanks you for correcting them. Touch 3 earns its send with a fact that didn't exist on day one: the venue moved to a bigger room, a blog quote ran, a chart position posted, another station added you. In the worked example, Marisol's Touch 3 carries the Queen City Beat quote and the room upgrade: "New since my last note, and then I'll go quiet: the premiere ran, and Foundry on Elm moved us to the big room — same night, April 17th. If it still isn't a fit, happy to drop it. If it is, the 3:24 cold ending sweeps clean." Then she goes quiet. That "then I'll go quiet" sentence is a promise the calendar keeps for her, and it's the most respectful sentence in the whole cadence.

Now the mechanics, because week two has its own rhythm. Day eight: Touch 2 to the twelve non-responders in the example — the send log tells you exactly who qualifies, which is why the log was never optional. Days nine through eleven: the reply window, and this is the week replies arrive if they're coming — Dara Whitfield answers the hook email within twenty-four hours with the add. Handle each one the Module 05 way: same-day, one step, no second ask riding along, thank-you out the door inside the hour. Day ten is also no-handling day: the clean no arrives — Sam at WDLX, "not for current rotation, try me in the fall" — and gets the one-line thank-you, the door explicitly held open, the fall date calendared. A clean no is a campaign asset. It's a station that now knows you do it right.

Days twelve and thirteen are quiet on purpose — buffer days that absorb real life, protect the cadence's dignity, and give your live threads room to breathe without you hovering. Day fourteen: Touch 3 to whoever remains silent — nine rows in the example — and then the sending phase of this campaign is finished. Done. Whatever happens after day fourteen happens in relationship mode: replies, confirmations, thank-yous, the interview scheduled for day sixteen. Between day fourteen and day twenty-one you send nothing new to anyone. That restraint is not lost opportunity; it's what the last week is made of.

The psychology needs saying plainly, because day ten is the loneliest day in this course. You will have sent thirty-odd emails total and received maybe four human replies, and the math will feel brutal if you let the silence set the exchange rate. So set it yourself, in advance, with the honest numbers: a twenty-percent human reply rate on a first campaign in a real market is a strong result. Twenty percent of twenty doors is four humans — and in the example, those four humans become one add, one interview, one imaging ask, and one fall door. Four humans is a career cell. It is not a participation trophy; it is the compounding seed of Module 06, and every campaign you run after this one starts with those relationships warm.

Two cadence corruptions to name and ban. First, the informal audit of Touch 2 replies: if a hook-email reply says "cool, thanks" and nothing else, that's a nurture, not a thread — status it, log it, and resist the urge to convert gratitude into an ask. Second, the fourth touch, in any disguise: "saw you opened it," "last one I promise," the LinkedIn follow, the DM. The cadence is three touches because three is what respect and statistics agree on. After three, you are done with this door until the next release, and the discipline that hurts today is the reason this same door opens in June.

One boundary case deserves its own paragraph, because it will happen to you: the reply that lands after Touch 3, in the quiet week. A host who answers on day seventeen after three touches and twenty days of silence — this is common, because your Touch 3 sat in a busy inbox until a Friday playlist meeting surfaced it. The protocol: respond same day, warmly, with zero references to how long it took. "So glad this found you — the offer stands" energy, deliver whatever they ask, and log the arrival lag in your notes, because reply-lag data tells you when market two's touches should actually land. The cadence has no pride. It only has a calendar.

The drill, at whatever day of week two you're on: draft Touch 2 and Touch 3 now — both of them, today, while you're calm — into the swipe file, so that the anxious version of you on day nine inherits a finished weapon instead of an open loop. Then execute the calendar. Done, at week's end, is a log that shows twelve Touch 2s and nine Touch 3s, every reply answered same-day, and a war room whose statuses tell the truth. That's the machine, mid-cycle, humming.

### Template

```
“[Name] — a 30-second version if that’s easier: [hook link]. The chorus lands by [0:52]. Still a fit for [show] before [date]? Either way — [one true sentence about their show].”

Touch 3 (day 14) — subject: New info: [fact] + “[song]”
“[Name] — new since my last note, and then I’ll go quiet: [new fact 1], and [new fact 2]. If “[song]” still isn’t a fit, happy to drop it. If it is, [one clock fact].”
```

## Common failure → fix

**Fails:** “Just bumping this to the top of your inbox” — Touch 2 with no new information, which converts your patience into their guilt.

**Fix:** Every touch carries something that didn't exist on day one: the 30s hook (T2), a quote/chart/room change (T3). New information is the toll the cadence charges. No new info? No send.
