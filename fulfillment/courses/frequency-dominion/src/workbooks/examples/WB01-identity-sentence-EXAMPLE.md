# WB01 — Identity Sentence · FILLED EXAMPLE

> EXAMPLE ONLY — Marisol Vega, every station, every person, every show, and every number below are fictional, built for training. Never copy example stations or “proof” into your own campaign. Real contacts come from your Radio Reach Vault, and your proof lines must be your own true facts.

**Artist:** Marisol Vega — midnight-synth — Cincinnati, OH

## The five drafts (thinking shown, not just the winner)

**Draft 1:** “I’m a synth-pop artist from Cincinnati chasing my dream.”
→ Dead on arrival. Could fit any artist in any city. No station, no job, no clock.

**Draft 2:** “I’m an electronic artist making retro music.”
→ Genre vague, city gone, listener feeling absent. Still a fan’s sentence.

**Draft 3:** “I’m a Cincinnati synth artist for late-night radio.”
→ Closer. ‘Late-night radio’ is a real daypart. Still no runtime, no proof of fit.

**Draft 4:** “I’m the Cincinnati synth artist specialty shows use for Friday 10pm blocks — 3:24, hook at 0:38.”
→ Almost. Daypart, clock, and structure. Missing the emotional cue a host can say on air.

**Draft 5:** “I’m the midnight-synth artist Cincinnati specialty shows call when they need a local 3:24 that sounds like rain-slick 1987 Berlin and lands its hook before the second chorus.”
→ PINNED. Insult test passed: no other artist on earth fits this sentence.

## Pinned sentence

> I’m the midnight-synth artist Cincinnati specialty shows call when they need a local 3:24 that sounds like rain-slick 1987 Berlin and lands its hook before the second chorus.

**Why it survives the insult test:** genre (midnight-synth), city (Cincinnati), clock (3:24), structure (hook before chorus 2), and a simile a host can steal for on-air patter (“rain-slick 1987 Berlin”). No other artist on earth fits this sentence.
