# M3L3 — EPK that does not embarrass you

**Module 03 · The Pitch Weapon** · 9 min lesson (1,042 words of spoken script ≈ 8 min at teleprompter pace)

**Outcome:** One page: who, where, what it sounds like, where to play it, who already did — nothing else.

---

## Voiceover script (read this one — teleprompter / VO)

Nobody wants your press kit. They want your fact sheet. The distinction is worth money, so let me draw it precisely. A press kit is a monument to you: the bio with the origin story, the four photo shoots, the "press" section with a podcast nobody's heard of. A fact sheet is a tool for them: who, where, what it sounds like, where to play it, who already did. Directors are building a show on a deadline. Monuments get admired. Tools get used.

So the EPK in this course is one page. Not one page plus a fold-out — one page, designed to be read in twenty seconds and printed if someone's studio still has a printer. The page has seven fields, in this order. Who: name, genre in four words a host can say on air, city. What: the song, runtime, structure facts — hook timing, cold ending, radio-clean status. Sounds like: two references maximum, dial-adjacent, phrased as a programming decision: "Depeche Mode next to Charli XCX, nobody reaches for the dial." Why now: the local date, the venue, the cap, the reason this week isn't someday. Proof: four lines, Module 01's stack, verbatim. Files: one link, folder with WAV, MP3, 30-second hook, this sheet as PDF. The ask: one sentence, same as the pitch's block six, exit included.

Every field earns its place by answering a question the director actually has. If a field answers a question only your mother asks, it's off the page. Your journey is off the page. Your influences are off the page — "sounds like" is a programming tool, not a tribute. Your full discography is off the page; the current single is the campaign, and the back catalogue is one line in the files folder if they want it.

The delivery format, non-negotiable: one link that resolves to everything. Not an attachment — attachments choke inboxes and die in spam filters. Not a zip — zips are where curiosity goes to starve. Not "streaming everywhere" with a scavenger hunt of platform logos. One clean URL — your domain or a simple link page — that opens on a phone to a folder: the WAV, the MP3, the 30-second hook, the one-sheet PDF. The example campaign's link is vega.link/staticbloom. That's the whole infrastructure, and it cost nothing and took an hour. If a director cannot get from your email to a broadcast-ready file in two taps, you don't have an EPK; you have a hobby.

Design rules, and I'm going to be blunt because this is where EPKs go to die. One typeface for headings, one for body. Black on white or near-white — the radio-read version is a document, not a poster; the ink-and-acid version lives in your press shots, not your fact sheet. One photo, current, sharp at thumbnail size, no fork-lighting mystery. Cover art for the single next to the song title — because art is genre information at a glance. Spellcheck like your add depends on it, because one "recieve" and the whole sheet whispers amateur hour. The one-sheet template in the zip gives you the exact structure; fill it, print it to PDF, done.

Where do you host the one link? Three honest options, in order of preference: a page on your own domain — one page, one folder, forever; a reputable link-in-bio tool with custom branding if you already run one; or a plain cloud folder with a clean share URL as a last resort. What disqualifies a host: autoplaying something that isn't the song, requiring login to download, expiring links on a schedule, or wrapping your art in their ads. Test your link in a private browser window monthly during campaign season. Dead links mid-campaign cost adds — and if one ever dies, the replacement-link subject line (number eight in your eleven) exists precisely for the repair.

Now the twenty-second test, which is the drill's acceptance bar. Hand your one-pager — or screen-share it — to a civilian. Someone who doesn't know your music. Twenty seconds. Then take the sheet away and ask three questions: what genre, what city, what's the ask. If they miss any of the three, the sheet failed where it must never fail. Revise until a stranger can pass your sheet. This is a painful test and the pain is the point: directors are strangers with less patience than yours.

Two advanced notes for when the basics are done. First, keep the sheet versioned per campaign: the proof lines refresh each cycle as the ledger grows — Marisol's April sheet says "spinning on 89.1 WVMR" where her March sheet said "KCYQ last fall." Same skeleton, new receipts. Second, the one-sheet doubles as your media kit for venues and sync clients in Module 06 — one artifact, three markets, which is exactly why it's built this clean.

What about photos and stage plot and tech rider, you ask, for the live side? Different document, different lesson, different buyer — venues want the stage plot and draw numbers, and that's the Module 06 venue email's job. The EPK answers one buyer: the person choosing what to play on the air this week. One page, one buyer, one ask. Everything else is a different weapon for a different door.

One last calibration, because I've reviewed a thousand of these sheets and the failure is always the same shape: the artist uses the proof section to apologize — "small but growing," "just starting to press," hedging every line into mush. Your numbers are your numbers. 2,400 monthly listeners with 61% in Ohio is a fact a Cincinnati programmer can actually use; "hopefully breaking out soon" is a wish wearing a lab coat. State facts at their actual size and let the sheet's cleanliness carry the confidence. The station is buying what's true, and a page that respects truth at any scale reads like an operator. That's the whole brand of this course in one PDF.

The drill: build the one-pager from the template — seven fields, one link, one photo, print-to-PDF. Then run the twenty-second civilian test and revise until they pass. Done means one artifact: a one-page PDF you'd send to your dream station without flinching. That sheet is going on every A-list send starting day one.

## Drill — done when

Build the EPK one-pager (template in the zip). Run the 20-second civilian test. Revise until a stranger can pass it.

**Done when:** Done = a one-page PDF exists with all seven fields + one working link, and your test civilian answered genre, city, and ask correctly. Screenshot the sheet.

### Template

```
WHO: [Artist] — [genre, four words] — [City, State]
WHAT: “[Song]” — [runtime edit] / [runtime album] · hook at [0:38] · cold ending · radio-clean
SOUNDS LIKE: [ref 1] × [ref 2] — “nobody reaches for the dial”
WHY NOW: [venue, cap, ticketed, date]
PROOF: 1) [date/cap] 2) [prior add] 3) [quote ≤8 words] 4) [listeners + regional %]
FILES: [one link] — WAV · MP3 · 30s hook · this sheet
ASK: One spin on [show] before [date]. A one-line no is enough.
```

## Common failure → fix

**Fails:** The EPK is a bio with feelings — origin story, influences, discography — and a director can’t find the runtime or the ask in 20 seconds.

**Fix:** Seven fields, one page, one link, one ask. Run the 20-second civilian test on a stranger and revise until they can answer genre, city, and ask.
