# M6L4 — The 90-day dominion review

**Module 06 · Empire Loop** · 8 min lesson (1,012 words of spoken script ≈ 8 min at teleprompter pace) · **Day 90**

**Outcome:** Audit sends, replies, adds, and burned wells — kill what doesn't convert, double what does.

## Voiceover script (read this one — teleprompter / VO)

Day ninety. Or the first quiet Sunday after your campaign closes — the trigger matters less than the ritual. This is the lesson that turns you from someone who ran a course into someone who runs a system, because systems are the only things in this business that improve with time, and they only improve when somebody reads the gauges. Ninety days is the interval: long enough for three campaigns or one campaign plus its market-two follow-through, short enough that you remember what the numbers felt like.

The review is six numbers and one verdict. Number one: sends — raw volume, and I mean raw; total emails is a vanity metric that earns its place only as the denominator for everything else. Number two: personalization rate — what fraction of your sends carried a true personal first line? Below eighty percent, the oath is leaking, and leaks compound into burned wells. Number three: replies — human ones only; auto-responders and "cool thanks" are logged but not counted. Number four: adds and interviews — the conversion nouns, the wins the ledger already holds. Number five: burned wells — any contact that got more than the cadence, any door that closed; this number should be zero, and the review's first job is diagnosing why it isn't. Number six, and it's the one artists forget to compute: reply rate by format and by subject-line shape — where did humans actually answer? That's the pattern the whole audit exists to surface.

Then the kill-or-double pass, which is where the review earns money. Format rule: any format under two percent reply after forty sends — cumulative across campaigns, not one — gets demoted: template-tight only, or nurture, or cut from the war room entirely. Forty sends is the threshold because it's the first count where a rate means anything; three silence-drowns is a mood, forty is a market verdict. Subject rule: whichever of your three keeper shapes opens highest becomes next campaign's default, and the worst shape gets replaced by a new candidate from the eleven. Door rule: any station that has now received three campaigns without a single human reply moves to nurture — quarterly news only, forever or until the host changes, because the Vault notes will tell you when the chair empties. And the double-down rule: whatever format, proof line, and ask shape produced your wins gets next campaign's A-game budget — the 30-second hook first, the phoner offer first, the day-one slot.

In the worked example, the numbers read: twenty sends, twelve Touch 2s, nine Touch 3s, four human replies — twenty percent — one add, one interview, one imaging ask, one clean no, zero burned wells, fourteen silences. The verdict paragraph is the part I want you to study, because it's the model for how a professional reads their own scoreboard: one add per twenty sends is a real, honest first campaign in a mid-size market — not a jackpot, not a failure — and the win is bigger than the spin: five ledger rows, one warm door in Columbus, zero burned wells, and a war room that is now a repeatable machine. Market two starts from higher ground. Notice what's absent from that paragraph: adjectives about feelings, verdicts about talent, any sentence that begins "radio just doesn't..." Numbers, interpretation, next action. That's the register.

The ninety-day horizon also catches what twenty-one days can't see: the slow metrics. Did the April add still get spins in June — specialty charts have long tails, and a song that holds eight weeks changes your pitch's proof line from "added" to "still charting," which is a different weapon. Did the warm doors stay warm — did Sam's "fall" date come due, did the almosts with their attached dates convert on schedule? Did the ledger's reuse column actually get used — if rows sat unspent, your pitches aren't drawing on the bank, and that's a habit fix, not a strategy problem. And the relationship roster: which humans on your wall now answer your emails inside a week? That list has a name — your champions — and by month three it should have entries, because champions are the compounding asset underneath all the numbers.

Schedule the review like it's a gig, because that's the only way it happens: ninety days out, on the calendar, titled, with the six numbers pre-written as column headers in the audit page of your log. Bring three artifacts: the send log, the ledger, and the war room. Close the door. One hour, once a quarter. The artists who do this outnumber — in results, not effort — the artists who don't, by a margin that embarrasses talent, because talent without gauges runs rich and steers blind.

One last thing, and it's the right note to end six modules on. The review's final question isn't about radio at all. It's: did the system make the music more likely? That's the entire point of Frequency Dominion — not radio for its own sake, but a machine quiet enough, small enough, and honest enough that it runs in forty-five-minute blocks and leaves the rest of your life for the actual work. If the calendar fought your writing, we shrink the war room. If the sends crowded the studio, we extend the cycle. The system serves the songs, never the reverse — and the day you feel that inversion, you have my permission, my instruction actually, to cut the campaign back to ten doors and go make the record. Dominion is the means. The music is the country.

The drill, and it's the last one: schedule your ninety-day review on your actual calendar right now — date, hour, the six numbers in the header. If a campaign just closed for you, run a mini-review today instead: compute the six numbers, write the verdict paragraph, choose one kill and one double-down. Done means the calendar holds the date or the log holds the verdict. That's the course. Twenty-four lessons, one machine, one loop — and from here, every cycle starts from higher ground. Go run it.

## Drill — done when

Schedule the 90-day review with the six numbers as column headers — or run a mini-review today if a campaign just closed.

### Template

```
Audit: sends ___ · personalization % ___ · human replies ___ · adds/interviews ___ · burned wells ___ · reply rate by format/subject ___
Verdict: numbers → interpretation → next action (no adjectives about talent).
```

## Common failure → fix

**Fails:** Judging the campaign on day 10 silence — or never computing the numbers at all, so every cycle runs on vibes.

**Fix:** The 40-send rule: rates under 40 sends are moods. Calendar the 90-day review with pre-written headers; kill what's under 2% after 40, fund whatever answered.
