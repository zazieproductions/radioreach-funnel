# M6L1 — Proof compounding

**Module 06 · Empire Loop** · 8 min lesson (1,071 words of spoken script ≈ 8 min at teleprompter pace)

**Outcome:** Turn every add, spin, quote, and interview into a ledger line you spend in the next pitch.

---

## Voiceover script (read this one — teleprompter / VO)

Here's the sentence this whole module hangs on: without a ledger, you restart from zero every single. Every release, every campaign, every pitch written from scratch, from nothing, from hope. With a ledger, every campaign starts from higher ground than the last one, automatically, because the wins are stored where the next pitch can reach them. That's the difference between a hobby with releases and a career with gravity — one document, kept honestly, forever.

The proof ledger is deliberately simple, because simple survives. Five columns: date, station and city, what happened — be exact — and where you'll reuse it. That last column is the one people skip and the one that makes the ledger an asset instead of a diary. "WVMR added Static Bloom, March 27" is a memory. "WVMR added Static Bloom, March 27 — goes in pitch line one for market two and the venue email badge" is ammunition with an address. Every entry knows its own next job.

Exactness is the standard, so let me define it. "Got radio play" is worthless. "Three-week specialty run on Lowlight, KCYQ 91.7 Columbus, Wednesday 11pm" is money — it names the station, the show, the slot, the duration, and it lets the next pitch say "my last single ran three weeks on Columbus specialty radio" with a straight face. Chart position with date. Quote with outlet and date, trimmed to eight words. Interview with show and air date. Numbers with the regional percentage. Every entry should be quotable in someone else's email without editing, because that's exactly what's going to happen to it.

Now watch the compounding, because this is the part that changes careers, and the worked example is the cleanest demonstration I can give you. September: Marisol's fall single runs three weeks in Columbus. That's ledger row one. March, next campaign: row one becomes proof line two in the pitch that lands the WVMR add. The WVMR add is ledger rows two and three — the spin and the chart placement. The WCBX interview is row four. The imaging ask is row five. April: five rows. June, next single: the pitch's proof line one is now "spinning on 89.1 WVMR, chart #7, three weeks" — an opening line the September version of Marisol could not have sent, could not have bought, and did not yet deserve. Same artist, same market, permanently higher floor. That's proof compounding. It is the single most reliable mechanism in this entire course, and it runs on documentation, not talent.

The ledger also disciplines your honesty, which protects everything else. Because every line gets reused in writing, in front of professionals, forever, the ledger cannot afford a single invented entry — one fabricated add discovered by a director who checks (they check) burns the real four rows around it. The ledger's truth standard is brutal by design: if you can't screenshot it, chart-page it, or point to the air date, it doesn't get a row. Feelings don't compound. Facts do.

Screenshot hygiene, the unglamorous craft skill of this lesson: capture the evidence the day it exists. Chart pages vanish weekly. Stream metadata rotates. Social posts get deleted by their hosts. The rule is same-day capture — spin airs tonight, screenshot goes in the ledger folder tonight, named with the date. You're building a private archive of your own career's receipts, and future you — applying for a festival, pitching a sync supervisor, filling out a venue's "press and radio history" form — will draw on it like a well. A two-year-old ledger with forty rows is one of the most powerful documents an independent artist can own. I've watched venue bookers go quiet reading one, in the good way.

Where the ledger feeds, in order of speed. The pitch, first — proof lines rotate in per Module 03's format rules. The EPK, second — the proof block refreshes every campaign, which is why it's versioned. The venue email, third — Module 06 lesson two turns ledger rows into room-negotiating badges. The sync and client pitches, fourth — radio facts translate to regional data that supervisors can actually use. The grant and festival applications, fifth — "charting on four specialty shows across three markets in twelve months" is the exact sentence those forms are hoping you can write. One document, five markets, zero extra work at capture time — ten minutes a week, and the interest runs forever.

The failure mode isn't dramatic; it's a slow leak. You get the add, you celebrate, you mean to write it down, the week eats you, and by June the spin is a warm blur — "I think it was the last week of March?" — too vague to spend. The antidote is the campaign rhythm you already own: the proof patrol. Day fifteen of every campaign is a patrol day — sweep the charts, the inboxes, the social mentions, and file every new fact with its reuse address. Five minutes on the calendar, once a campaign, and the ledger stays current without ever becoming a chore. Tie it to the Sunday audit if that's easier to remember — patrol then photo, one ritual, one wall.

One deeper point before the drill, about what the ledger does to your psychology across years. Artists without receipts are at the mercy of each release's mood — the quiet single feels like collapse because there's no memory of ground already gained. Artists with ledgers can see the floor rising: four rows after campaign one, nine after campaign two, and the graph only goes one direction, because even the silences and nos get logged as market intelligence. The ledger converts a career from a series of emotional events into a compounding structure, and structures don't need you to feel hopeful to keep working. Hope is a nice place to visit. A ledger is a place to live.

The drill: create the ledger today — the template ships in the zip, five columns, one sheet — and backfill every true win you already have, however small: the campus show, the blog quote, the community station spin from two years ago, the playlist add with a name attached. Even a three-row ledger beats a blank page, and three true rows is genuinely enough to start this machine. Done means the sheet exists with at least one backfilled row and its reuse address written. The ledger is the empire's bank. Start the deposit tonight.

## Drill — done when

Create the proof ledger and backfill every true win — one row minimum, reuse address included.

**Done when:** Done = the ledger sheet exists with ≥1 backfilled row and a written 'where I'll reuse it' address. Screenshot row one.

### Template

```
Ledger row: DATE | STATION + CITY | WHAT HAPPENED (exact: show, slot, weeks, position) | WHERE YOU'LL REUSE IT
Truth standard: screenshot it, chart-page it, or point to the air date — or it doesn't get a row.
```

## Common failure → fix

**Fails:** The slow leak — the add happens, the week eats you, and by June the spin is a warm blur too vague to spend.

**Fix:** The proof patrol: day 15 of every campaign, sweep charts/inboxes/mentions and file every fact with its reuse address. Same-day screenshots, named with the date.
