Is RadioReach right for your situation?

By the RadioReach editorial team Published August 19, 2026 Reviewed August 19, 2026

RadioReach is the right tool if you intend to run your own radio outreach and need organized contact research to do it. It is the wrong tool if you want a campaign managed for you, playlist placement, curator feedback, or any guarantee of airplay. The table below states which situation you are in, and what to use instead when the answer is "not this".

Where it genuinely helps

The value of a contact file is narrow and specific: it collapses the research phase of a radio campaign. Artists routinely spend weeks assembling a target list by hand — searching stations, reading staff pages, guessing at address formats, recording it all in a spreadsheet. That work is real, unpaid, and identical for every artist who does it. Buying it pre-done is worth $20 to a lot of people.

What it does not do is make anyone want to play your record. The pitch, the song and the fit still decide that, and our free pitch templates and step-by-step guide cover that half of the work at no cost.

Where it will disappoint you

Be honest with yourself about the time question in particular. A contact file is leverage for someone who will sit down and send fifty personalised emails over three weeks. For someone who will not, it is a spreadsheet that gets opened once. The research bottleneck is only the bottleneck if the sending is not.

Equally, if you came looking for a guarantee, no honest seller can give you one. Buying a contact list does not guarantee airplay. Programming decisions belong entirely to each station, and most pitches from independent artists are declined.

Limitations of this page

This guide is written by RadioReach about its own product, so read it as a self-assessment rather than an independent review. We have tried to be accurate about where the product fails, because a refund and a bad review cost us more than a skipped sale — but we are not a neutral party, and you should weigh it accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Is RadioReach worth it for a first release?

It depends on your time, not your career stage. If you will personally send and follow up on pitches over the next month, the research shortcut is worth $20. If this is your first release and you are still finishing the record, our free step-by-step guide is the better starting point.

Should I buy this or hire a radio promoter?

Buy the file if you want control and have time; hire a promoter if you want the work done and have budget in the four figures. They solve different problems. Our comparison guide walks through the trade-off, including the red flags of a bad promoter.
