Contact database vs managed campaign

By the RadioReach editorial team Published August 19, 2026 Reviewed August 19, 2026

Buy a contact database if the bottleneck is knowing who to contact. Buy a managed campaign if the bottleneck is having anyone to do the contacting. A database is cheap, reusable and useless without your labour. A managed campaign is expensive, single-use and does the labour. Diagnose which bottleneck is actually yours before spending anything.

Diagnose the bottleneck

Most wasted promotion money comes from misdiagnosis. An artist who has never sent a pitch buys a campaign and cannot evaluate whether it was run well. An artist with no free hours buys a spreadsheet and never opens it twice.

The test is simple. If someone handed you a perfect list of fifty correct radio contacts tomorrow, would you send fifty personalised emails and follow up twice over the next month? If yes, the database is your answer and the campaign is overspending. If no, the database will not help you, and no amount of data quality changes that.

The hybrid most artists land on

The common sequence is to start self-directed at the accessible tier, learn what a working pitch looks like and which stations respond, then hire help once there is a track record worth amplifying and a release with budget behind it. Doing it in that order means that when you do hire a promoter, you can evaluate their work, because you know what the job involves.

Limitations of this page

Cost ranges here are general industry characterisations, not survey data. "Four figures" for managed campaigns varies enormously by scope and market. RadioReach sells a contact database, so weigh this comparison accordingly.

Frequently asked questions

Is a radio contact database worth it?

It is worth it if you will personally send and follow up on pitches — it removes weeks of research for a one-time cost. It is not worth it if you lack the time to do outreach, because a database does no outreach for you. Our fit guide works through the scenarios.
