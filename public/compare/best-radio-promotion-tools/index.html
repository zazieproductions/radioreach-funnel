Radio promotion tools compared

By the RadioReach editorial team Published August 19, 2026 Reviewed August 19, 2026

There is no single best radio promotion tool, and any page claiming otherwise is selling something — including this one, which is published by a company that sells one of these options. The categories below solve different problems: contact research, submission platforms, managed promotion, and tracking. Pick by matching the category to your bottleneck, then compare products within that category.

Choosing between them

Work backwards from the specific thing blocking you. If you do not know which stations to approach, buy research. If you know who to approach but get no replies, the problem is usually the pitch or the record rather than the list, and a submission platform at least tells you which. If you have neither time nor relationships and do have budget, hire a promoter. If you are already getting played and cannot prove it, buy tracking.

Sequence matters more than product choice. A press kit before outreach, a target list before pitching, tracking after placements. Buying a later-stage tool to solve an earlier-stage problem is the most common way artists waste money on promotion.

Claims to distrust

  • Guaranteed airplay. Buying a contact list does not guarantee airplay. Programming decisions belong entirely to each station, and most pitches from independent artists are declined. No tool or service in any category can promise it.
  • Enormous contact counts with no methodology. Ask how the list was compiled, when, and what the seller means by "verified" — see our own definitions for the standard we hold ourselves to.
  • "Industry secret" framing. Station contact information is largely published by stations, because stations want submissions.
  • Placement counts without station names. Checkable adds or nothing.
  • Comparison pages with no disclosed author. Including, in fairness, the ones that rank above this page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best radio promotion tool for independent artists?

There isn't one — the right category depends on your bottleneck. Contact research helps if you don't know who to pitch; submission platforms help if you want guaranteed replies; promoters help if you lack time and relationships. Match the tool to the specific problem rather than to marketing claims.

Are free radio contact lists good enough?

Free lists can work, but they are usually out of date, incomplete, or missing the role and name detail that makes a pitch land. The real cost is your time spent verifying them. Whether paying to skip that is worth it depends on how much outreach you plan to do.
