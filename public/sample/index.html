Why no real names or addresses? Publishing a station employee's name and email on a public marketing page invites exactly the mass, untargeted emailing that damages artists' reputations with stations — and it would misrepresent people as endorsing RadioReach. The paid file exists for researched, individual outreach. The sample exists to show you the structure before you buy.

Station types and geography

The file is weighted toward the parts of radio that realistically play independent music. In practice that means college, community and public stations form the bulk of it, with commercial stations included primarily through specialty and mixshow programming rather than main rotation.

  • College and university radio
  • Community and noncommercial radio
  • Public radio
  • Commercial radio
  • Specialty and genre-specific programs

Primary coverage is United States. Australia (paid regional add-on) is available as a separate regional add-on. We do not currently publish per-state or per-country counts, because those numbers have not been measured against the shipping export with a reproducible script — see the methodology page for why we would rather publish nothing than publish an estimate.

How complete the data is

Set your expectations correctly before you buy, because the most common disappointment with any contact file is an expectation mismatch:

  • Not every row has a named person. Many stations publish only a submissions inbox, and for those stations the inbox is the correct route.
  • Not every row has a direct email. Some stations take submissions only through a form, and the record points you at it.
  • Some records will be out of date by the time you use them. Radio contacts change constantly through staff turnover, ownership changes, format shifts and station closures. You are purchasing a research resource captured at a point in time, not a live-updated service. Confirm a contact on the station's own website before you pitch.
  • Inclusion is not permission. A station being listed does not mean it accepts unsolicited music. Read its submission page first.

Why this sample is structural rather than a data dump

A free slice of live contacts would be the more persuasive marketing move, and we are choosing not to make it. Handing out working addresses publicly guarantees they get blasted, which burns the contact for every artist including our own buyers, and it exposes individuals who published a work address for careful enquiries rather than bulk mail. What you can evaluate from this page — field structure, station mix, completeness, and limitations — is what actually determines whether the file is useful to you.

Limitations of this page

This sample illustrates structure, not scale. The rows above are constructed examples of real station categories; they are not extracted verbatim from the file, and the named stations are used illustratively rather than as a claim about which specific contacts the file holds for them. No station named on this page is affiliated with or endorses RadioReach. Coverage figures are described qualitatively because they have not yet been measured with a published script.

Frequently asked questions

Can I see the real contact list before buying?

Not the live records. This page shows the field structure, station mix and completeness so you can judge fit, but publishing working contacts publicly would get them mass-emailed and burn them for everyone. If the file is not useful once you have it, the 30-day refund applies and you keep the file.

Does every contact include a person's name and direct email?

No, and any list claiming otherwise is overstating itself. Where a station publishes a named music or program director, the record carries them. Where a station publishes only a submissions inbox or a form, the record carries that route instead, because that is genuinely the correct way to reach that station.

What format is the file delivered in?

A formatted PDF document plus city-sorted contact tables, delivered as a downloadable archive. You download it immediately after checkout and keep it permanently — there is no subscription and no renewal.
