How to build a music director contact list

By the RadioReach team Updated August 19, 2026 2 min read Templates & press kits

On this page
  • Know the decision-making roles
  • A repeatable research method
  • Segment the list before outreach
  • Pitch a music director professionally
  • Maintain the contact list
  • Measure list quality, not just volume

A music director contact list is valuable only when it maps a release to the person who can evaluate it. Titles vary, staff turns over, and many stations distribute music decisions across genre directors or individual hosts. Building the list therefore means documenting roles and programming fit—not collecting every address attached to a station domain.

Know the decision-making roles

RoleWhen to use it
Music directorGeneral new-release review, adds, rotation, and often chart reporting.
Genre directorCollege or community submissions in a defined style such as hip-hop, jazz, or electronic.
Specialty-show hostMusic that fits one named program more closely than the station's overall format.
Program directorSmaller stations with combined roles or broader programming questions.
Submission inbox/formUse first whenever the station explicitly requires it.

A repeatable research method

  1. Open the official station site rather than relying on a search snippet.
  2. Read the staff, schedule, and submission pages together.
  3. Check a current playlist for genre fit.
  4. Capture name, exact role, official route, source URL, and date checked.
  5. Record any file, timing, clean-edit, or eligibility requirements.
  6. Flag uncertain records for confirmation rather than guessing an address.

Search results and social profiles can help resolve ambiguity, but the official station page should remain the primary source. Never infer personal email patterns or scrape addresses from unrelated contexts.

Segment the list before outreach

Create campaign views by station type, country, time zone, genre, local/tour relevance, and decision role. Add a “reason for fit” field that must be completed before send. If that field is blank, the contact is not ready for outreach.

Prioritize named genre and music directors whose playlists demonstrate fit. Place general submission routes in a second tier and program directors in a role-appropriate tier. This order reduces irrelevant email and keeps the strongest prospects from being buried in a giant sheet.

Pitch a music director professionally

Use the person's current name and title, reference one credible programming signal, describe the release in a precise genre lane, and provide one stable stream. Keep the first message short. Offer a clean edit, one-sheet, artwork, or WAV behind the link rather than attaching a large package.

Send two to three weeks before release unless the station says otherwise. Follow up once after seven to ten days. A nonresponse is not permission for an automated sequence.

Maintain the contact list

  • Timestamp every verification and response.
  • Remove hard bounces and suppress opt-outs immediately.
  • Update roles when an auto-reply or station page identifies a change.
  • Recheck priority records before every release.
  • Preserve positive relationships and thank confirmed supporters.
  • Never overwrite a suppression record with a fresh purchased import.

Measure list quality, not just volume

Track verified-role rate, playlist-fit rate, hard-bounce rate, reply rate, adds, spins, and opt-outs. A smaller list with current roles and low bounces is more valuable than a huge file with no provenance. Airplay remains an editorial outcome; the list improves preparation, not certainty.

Frequently asked questions

What does a radio music director do?

A music director reviews releases, manages adds and rotation, and may report charts. At smaller stations the program director, genre director, or individual show host may perform the same function.

Where can I find current music director contacts?

Start with official station staff, contact, submission, and show pages. College and community stations may also publish music office hours or genre-director listings. Verify third-party data against those sources.

Should I contact the program director instead?

Use the role closest to the decision. A music or genre director is usually best for new releases; a program director may be right at a small station where roles overlap. Specialty hosts choose their own shows at many stations.
