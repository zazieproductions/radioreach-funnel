How to Find Radio Stations Accepting Music Submissions

Searching for radio stations accepting music is less about finding one universal submission directory than learning how to recognize the stations, programs, and people that are open to independent releases. Submission policies can change, and even stations that accept music may only consider releases that fit their programming.

Start with station fit, not email volume

Before collecting addresses, narrow the field by genre, geography, format, audience, and programming. A smaller list of plausible stations is usually more useful than thousands of unrelated contacts.

Look at recently played artists, specialty shows, local-music programs, station charts, playlists, and submission pages. Those signals tell you whether a station is worth contacting before you spend time writing the pitch.

Which radio contacts matter?

Useful contacts can include music directors, program directors, genre directors, DJs, specialty-show producers, and dedicated submission addresses. The right person depends on how the station is organized.

A music director may screen new releases at one station while an individual DJ chooses music independently at another. That is why contact research and station research belong together.

What should a radio submission email contain?

Keep the first message easy to process. Identify yourself, explain briefly what you are sending, provide a streaming link, and give the recipient a reason the release could fit the station. Include essential release information without turning the email into a biography.

Personalized does not have to mean long. One accurate sentence demonstrating that you know what the station plays can be more persuasive than several paragraphs of generic promotion.

Build a repeatable radio outreach workflow

Keep records of the station, contact, submission date, response, and any follow-up. This prevents duplicate outreach and lets you learn which types of stations respond to your music.
