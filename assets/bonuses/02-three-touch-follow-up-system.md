# The 3-Touch Follow-Up System

**A Radio Reach Vault founder bonus.**
Most replies don't come from the first email. They come from the second — which is the
one almost nobody sends.

---

## The premise

A music director opens somewhere between 40 and 300 unsolicited emails a week. Yours
was not rejected. It was *not yet processed*. Those are completely different things,
and only one of them is worth acting on.

The follow-up is not nagging. The follow-up is the job.

**The rule that keeps you welcome:** three touches, then stop. Not four. Not "just
checking in one more time." Three, then you go quiet and you come back with your next
release. Stations remember the artists who knew when to stop, and they remember them
warmly.

---

## The cadence

| Touch | When | Length | What it contains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — The pitch** | Day 0 | ~80 words | Who you are, why this station, one link |
| **2 — The bump** | Day 4 | ~25 words | Same thread, no new information, zero pressure |
| **3 — The update** | Day 11 | ~40 words | Same thread, **one new fact** |
| **4 — The close** | Day 25 | ~30 words | Graceful exit, door left open |

Then stop. Next contact is your next release.

---

## Why these specific days

- **Day 4** — clears a normal inbox cycle without landing in the same week's noise. If
  you pitched Tuesday, this lands Saturday–Monday, when the queue is shortest.
- **Day 11** — long enough that you're not a pest, short enough that your original
  email is still in the thread and still findable.
- **Day 25** — most programming decisions for a given cycle are made by now. This one
  isn't really a pitch; it's relationship maintenance dressed as an email.

---

## Touch 2 — The bump (day 4)

Reply to your own sent email so the original stays attached. Do not restate your pitch.

> Hi [FIRST NAME] — floating this back up in case it got buried. Track's here if you
> get a minute: [LINK]
>
> Thanks either way.
> [NAME]

**Rules:** under 30 words. No new ask. No "I really think you'll love it." The entire
job of this email is to move the thread to the top of the inbox.

---

## Touch 3 — The update (day 11)

This one requires a new fact. If you don't have one, you don't send this email — you
skip to day 25.

Acceptable new facts:
- Another station added it
- It was added to a playlist (any playlist with real listeners)
- The video is out
- You announced a show in or near their market
- A local outlet wrote about it
- You have a session/acoustic version now

> Hi [FIRST NAME] — quick update: [NEW FACT].
>
> Still think it'd fit [SHOW/STATION] — here it is again: [LINK]
>
> [NAME]

**Why the new fact matters:** it converts your follow-up from "please notice me" into
"here is new information," which is a legitimate reason to be in someone's inbox. It
also quietly signals momentum, and programmers respond to momentum.

---

## Touch 4 — The close (day 25)

The highest-leverage email in this entire system, and the one artists never send.

> Hi [FIRST NAME] — I'll leave this one alone, I know the queue is long. If it's not a
> fit, no problem at all.
>
> I'll send the next one when it's ready. Thanks for the show.
>
> [NAME]

**What this does:** it removes all pressure, demonstrates you understand how their job
works, and pre-authorises your next email. A meaningful number of replies arrive
*because* of this email — it's the one that makes people feel comfortable answering.

---

## The reopener

For a thread that went completely silent months ago and you have a genuine reason to
revive:

> Hi [FIRST NAME] — different track, and I think a much better fit for [SHOW] than the
> last one I sent: [LINK]
>
> Out [DATE]. Thanks for the ear.
> [NAME]

Notice there is no apology, no "sorry to bother you again," no reference to being
ignored. You're simply a working artist with a new release. That's it.

---

## What to do with a "no"

A real "no" is worth more than silence. Reply once, in one line:

> Totally understood — thanks for actually replying, that's rare. I'll send the next
> one when it's out.

You have now converted a rejection into a warm contact for your next release. Mark them
in your tracker as **NO — re-pitch next release**, not as a dead end.

---

## Volume guardrails

- Cap yourself at **20–30 first-contact emails per session.** Beyond that the
  personalisation degrades, and unpersonalised outreach is worse than none.
- Never run this from an automation tool. One human, one send, every time.
- If a station's page says "no follow-ups," believe them and send only touch 1.
- If anyone asks you to stop contacting them, stop immediately and permanently.
